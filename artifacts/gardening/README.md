# Work Ledger Garden

The garden is an owner-controlled maintenance workflow. It reconciles ambiguous or stale mandatory state before making its representation smaller. It never autonomously decides what the ledger should mean.

`PROTOCOL.md` sections 7 and 26 are canonical. These files are prompts and record templates; they do not grant repository, source, Git, external-action, or semantic-adoption authority.

## Routine and heavy modes

- A **routine garden** responds to the derived byte warning, file-budget warnings, more than 25 open loops, or a noticed stale/duplicate route with a small answer-gated review.
- A **heavy garden** is due when the exact Protocol section-7 joined startup corpus reaches 40,000 measured `o200k_base` tokens or a documented consequential error is traced to stale, contradictory, or overcrowded mandatory context.

Exact `o200k_base` tokens are canonical. The post-split exact measurement
derives a routine byte-warning threshold at 36,000 tokens; release performs a
second exact measurement and recalibrates it. The byte threshold is only a
warning proxy, never the limit or proof of compliance. There is no word-count
fallback. If exact tokenization cannot run when required, cap compliance and
adoption remain incomplete; a documented consequential failure can still
select heavy mode.

Heavy mode is a dedicated Deep TRACER task. It freezes a baseline, maps owner answers, builds a reversible candidate, runs three separately reasoned semantic-conservation lenses, reruns affected work after revision, verifies durable routes and representative retrieval, and performs an unconditional final whole-candidate audit before owner adoption. An adoption-ready candidate must measure below the 40,000-token context limit. If semantic conservation prevents that, the garden retains meaning and reports a blocked over-limit state; the limit never authorizes semantic pruning.

TRACER and the heavy audit are both required. The Deep trace supplies pre-action claim-dependency reasoning, premise/link assessment, challenge, stance, memory delta, and dependency review. The heavy audit supplies whole-diff coverage, logic/identity conservation, and provenance/retrieval conservation. Neither replaces the other.

The first garden question in every interactive routine or heavy garden is one
bounded TRACER card. It shows the exact included claim assessments, retrieval
and coverage limits, and material graph
links for owner confirmation or correction, including an explicit zero-claim
form for zero relevant registered claims, distinguishing legacy or unregistered
prose. The card counts within the eight-question limit. No answer makes no
semantic change, confirmation alone does not churn versions, and an actual
claim or relationship change uses the protected staged-tree procedure and
substantive review.

After the claim card, the garden checks workstream staleness before other
questions. Dossier declarations remain permissive. Staleness fires only when a
declaring dossier has exact `Dossier status: complete YYYY-MM-DDTHH:mm:ss.fffZ`
later than the row's exact UTC-millisecond `Last reviewed` timestamp, records
nonempty material impact on that stream's status, next action, routes, or claims,
and the row does not reflect it. Mere declaration, consultation, and mechanical work do not
fire. Several stale rows should be combined into one question when useful.

## Files

- `weekly-garden-prompt.md` — reminder-only wakeup text and complete interactive routine/heavy routing.
- `garden-proposal-template.md` — combined cycle record with conditional heavy-audit fields.
- `../../templates/garden-prompt.md` — generic reusable interactive prompt for a neutral ledger.
- `../../templates/heavy-garden-review.md` — generic heavy-garden review record.
- `garden-log.jsonl` and dated agendas — instance-local history, not neutral product seed data.

## Authority and safety boundaries

- An unattended reminder may only ask the owner to reply `Run the garden`; it performs no repository inspection, dossier creation, source retrieval, or mutation. Scheduling is user/surface configuration, makes no project-attached-task assumption, and may be replaced by a human calendar reminder.
- `Run the garden` authorizes the internal review only. External scouting requires separate authority.
- Garden-generated semantic changes require an exact owner answer. No answer means no semantic change.
- One coordinator writes. Reviewers remain read-only and provisional.
- Passing validation, fewer warnings, or getting below the context limit never establishes semantic conservation.
- Owner adoption and Git handoff are separate decisions.

Neutral builds include only the generic rule, prompts, templates, tests, and an empty or absent local log. They exclude prior owners' schedules, cycle records, questions, dossiers, measurements, and current-state decisions.
